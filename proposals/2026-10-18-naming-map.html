Studiolo · naming

Four names, plain words for everything else

Studiolo has four names of its own: Studiolo, Loci, Commonplace and Lumen. Each comes from the world of the Renaissance study. Everything else uses words a learner already knows. This page maps every part of the app to its name and the one line that says what it does.

Draft for the owner's decision · 18 Oct 2026

The four names

Studiolo stoo-dee-OH-loh · Italian, "little study"

A private study for everything you learn.

The app, and the assistant inside it. A studiolo was a Renaissance scholar's private room for books, objects and thinking.

Loci LOH-sigh · Latin, "places"

Your mind palace: everything you know, in one place you can walk through.

The map of your areas, topics and sub-topics, with your Commonplace and Lumens linked in. Named after the memory palace technique, the method of loci.

Commonplace KOM-un-plays · from Latin loci communes

Everything you gather, kept under your topics.

Your sources, documents, notes and quotes. Renaissance scholars kept a commonplace book: extracts filed under topic headings, the loci communes. Loci holds your topics; the Commonplace holds what you gathered under them. Was: Library.

Lumen LOO-men · Latin, "light"

Light on any topic, in a few narrated minutes.

A short narrated, cited slide video made from a topic or from something in your Commonplace. Lumens are also the parts of a course.

The copilot: call it Studiolo

Studiolo has one assistant, with six skills (product scope, "Graph copilot"). The app already says ✦ Ask Studiolo in some places and Copilot in others, and Settings already has Instructions for Studiolo. The simplest fix is to let the assistant be Studiolo itself. Learners ask Studiolo; they do not have to learn a fifth name.

Rules

  1. Four names of our own, no more. Studiolo, Loci, Commonplace, Lumen. A new feature gets a plain name unless the owner agrees otherwise.
  2. Capitals for names. Destinations and coined names are capitalised: Today, Loci, Commonplace, Learn, a Lumen. Everyday words are not: a topic, a note.
  3. Plurals and articles. Lumens, never Lumina. Loci is already plural and is a place: "in Loci", never "a Loci". In sentences say "your Commonplace"; on labels and buttons, just "Commonplace".
  4. Learner words, not system words. No "node", "graph", "item" (use the kind), "AI", "bot", "prompt" or "generate". Say topic, note, Ask Studiolo, make, explain.
  5. Verbs stay plain. Explain…, Capture, Connect. Coined names are never verbs: not "Lumen this".
  6. Code keeps its names. Routes, tables, events and analytics keep graph, library, explainer, copilot and content items. Only the words people see change.

Decisions for the owner

  1. Rename the Graph destination to Loci, with the 3D view named Palace and made the default. The alternative is to keep Graph and use Loci only for the 3D view.
  2. Rename Library to Commonplace. Test it with a few learners first: in everyday English "commonplace" also means ordinary. If it reads oddly, keep Library.
  3. Call the assistant Studiolo ("✦ Ask Studiolo") and retire "Copilot" in the interface.
  4. Adopt Lumen and Lumens for explainers, as in the 17 Oct proposal.
  5. Approve the skill names: Build my map, Find sources, Tidy my Commonplace, Plan a course, Teach me, Explain….
