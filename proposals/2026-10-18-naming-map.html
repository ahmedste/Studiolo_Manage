Studiolo · naming

One product, three names inside it, plain words for everything else

Studiolo is the product: a private study. Inside it are three places and things with names of their own, Loci, the Commonplace and Lumens, each from the world of the Renaissance study. Everything else uses words a learner already knows.

The three names inside Studiolo

Loci LOH-sigh · Latin, "places"

Your mind palace: everything you know, in one place you can walk through, and ask.

Your areas, topics and sub-topics, with your Commonplace and Lumens linked in. Named after the memory palace technique, the method of loci. It is also who you ask (see below).

Commonplace KOM-un-plays · from Latin loci communes

Everything you gather, kept under your topics.

Renaissance scholars kept a commonplace book: extracts filed under topic headings, the loci communes. Loci holds your topics; the Commonplace holds what you gathered under them.

Lumen LOO-men · Latin, "light"

Light on any topic, in a few narrated minutes.

A short narrated, cited slide video made from a topic or from something in your Commonplace. Lumens are also the parts of a course.

The assistant: Ask Loci

Studiolo has one assistant with six skills. Calling it "Ask Studiolo" would mean asking the whole product, like asking the building. Asking Loci means asking your mind palace: the place that holds everything you know, your topics, your Commonplace and your Lumens. It answers from what is there, and helps you put more in. No fifth name, no character to invent, and every answer comes back to your Loci.

Rules

  1. One product, three names inside. Studiolo, then Loci, Commonplace and Lumen. A new feature gets a plain name unless the owner agrees otherwise.
  2. Capitals for names. Destinations and our names are capitalised: Today, Loci, Commonplace, Learn, a Lumen. Everyday words are not: a topic, a note.
  3. Loci, the place and the voice. As a place: "in Loci", "your Loci", never "a Loci". As the assistant: "Ask Loci", "Loci suggests". Never "the Loci".
  4. Plurals and articles. Lumens, never Lumina. "Your Commonplace" in sentences; just "Commonplace" on labels and buttons.
  5. Learner words, not system words. No "node", "graph", "item" (use the kind), "AI", "bot", "copilot", "prompt" or "generate". Say topic, note, Ask Loci, make, explain.
  6. Code keeps its names. Routes, tables, events and analytics keep graph, library, explainer, copilot and content items. Only the words people see change.

Decisions for the owner

  1. Rename Graph to Loci, covering both the 2D Map and the 3D Palace, with Palace as the default view.
  2. Call the assistant Loci ("✦ Ask Loci", "Suggested by Loci", "Instructions for Loci") and retire "Copilot" and "Ask Studiolo".
  3. Rename Library to Commonplace. Test it with a few learners first: in everyday English "commonplace" also means ordinary. If it reads oddly, keep Library.
  4. Adopt Lumen and Lumens for explainers, as in the 17 Oct proposal.
  5. Approve the skill names: Build my map, Find sources, Tidy my Commonplace, Plan a course, Teach me, Explain….
